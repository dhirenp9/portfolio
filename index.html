Leadership approach

How I lead, in four commitments

I lead with empathy. Instead of resisting the flow of change, I guide it. I bring stakeholders and end users into the same room for visioning, build on systems thinking, and coach my designers to hold a clear point of view while listening to business and user needs alike. Throughout, design is the experience layer that keeps product, business, and engineering moving together.

Outcomes before output

Every initiative starts with a business goal and a customer opportunity. We run experience vision workshops with key cross-functional stakeholders and end users, so the goal and the experience are agreed before design starts.

Build systems, not heroics

The team designs with systems thinking. We start with an object model, move to object flows, and then define a UX framework before we build, so the quality of the work does not depend on me being in the room.

Grow people on purpose

I coach designers to hold a strong point of view on the experience and to be the experts in the room, while listening just as closely to business needs as to user needs. So far, I have hired 6 designers and 2 have been promoted.

Influence across the table

I champion alignment between product, business, and engineering. Design is the connective tissue, the experience layer that ties their goals together, and through visioning we help shape the roadmap.

Company-level Equinix Jan 2026 to now

Rebuilding the IBX ops platform experience, from low code to React

The IBX operations platform ran on low code, which limited usability and slowed every change. We rebuilt the experience in React and changed how the design team works at the same time: agents first, on top of our design system, research and service design principles. The goal was a better experience for the people using it, a shorter path from idea to shipped, and more transparency for the customer.

What I put in place
An agents-first operating model for the design team, built on three foundations: the Equinix design system, ongoing research, and service design principles. I created an agent that builds a fully functioning front end from React components, which engineering takes and connects to the back end. Design hands off working code instead of static specs.
How I managed to outcomes
I keep the team moving toward outcomes through a steady rhythm: staff calls to share context and priorities, 1:1s to coach and unblock each designer, and squad syncs to track progress on the work. When a difficult moment comes up, I start with alignment and open communication, and I escalate when that is not enough.

Initiative-level Global Ops design team Late Q3 2026 to now

Moving the design team to agent-first sprints

I introduced agent-first sprints so the team delivers designs with AI agents doing the heavy lifting, from first draft to working front end. Designers spend their time on judgment: framing the problem, setting the experience direction, and reviewing what the agents produce.

What I put in place
An agent-first sprint model grounded in our design system, research, and service design principles. Every sprint runs two to three weeks and moves through two phases. First we define: a visioning workshop, persona and role work, and concepting. Then we build: our agent generates production-ready React components that engineering connects to the back end. Weekly design reviews and continuous discovery keep quality high and the direction true.

Project-level Omnitracs 2019

Fuel Tax Application, the first app on a new platform

I was chosen to lead design on a back-office application for fleets filing IFTA (International Fuel Tax Agreement) taxes. Built with Red Hat, it was to be the first application on the new platform Omnitracs was creating, and it had to be responsive.

Define
An 8-week lab with Red Hat, in a pod of Red Hat and Omnitracs developers working offsite. We ran event storming and design thinking sessions to decide what to build and how.
Design
As the sole designer, I turned what we learned into the product vision: the information architecture, wireframes, and a prototype.
Validate
We ran usability and A/B tests on the prototype with users at our annual user conference.
Win buy-in
I built a persona from real fleet compliance managers and an animated walkthrough of the filing flow, then showed our C-level how the product would save users time on fuel taxes.
The team
Sole designer, working alongside Red Hat and Omnitracs developers.
Project-level Humandev 2017

Merlin, a conversational healthcare app

As a freelance UX/UI designer, I worked on a six-month project to streamline and triage communication between patients, clinics, and clinic staff through a conversational chatbot interface. I designed both the mobile and desktop experiences.

Research
I interviewed patients and doctors and built an affinity analysis from what they told us, so the product started from real needs.
Structure
From that analysis I created the information architecture and site map for both mobile and desktop.
Design
Sketches and wireframes, then interactive prototypes of the chatbot interactions.
Validate
We tested early and often with users, and used their feedback to improve the product.
Deliver
A UI kit and visual design that the development team used to build the product, which was ready for production at the end of the engagement.

About

Career so far

My path into design was not a straight line. I began in engineering, where I learned to break complex problems into their parts. Start-ups came next, and with them a bit of everything: wearing whatever hat the moment needed taught me how products really get built and how much a small team can do. Along the way I found design, and it stuck. It let me keep solving hard problems while keeping people at the center of the answer, and that pairing has guided my career ever since.
